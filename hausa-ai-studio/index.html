01 · The hidden risk

Hausa can look fluent while still being wrong.

For non-Hausa teams, the dangerous outputs are often the fluent ones. They sound plausible, but miss meaning, tone, culture, safety, or dataset usefulness.

Fluent wording is not the same as usable Hausa.

Good Hausa AI and localization work needs native judgment, cultural context, quality scoring, and production-minded issue reporting.

1Meaning drift: dropped nuance, wrong tense, literal transfer, or named entity mistakes hidden inside smooth Hausa.
2Register mismatch: wording that is too formal, cold, imported, or unnatural for the real user context.
3AI risk: hallucinated facts, unsafe certainty, weak refusal behavior, or overconfident Hausa explanations.
4Data risk: noisy transcripts, inconsistent labels, weak prompt-response pairs, and evaluation examples that cannot be trusted.

05 · Quality rubric

A review system buyers can understand

The studio uses practical severity levels and review dimensions that turn Hausa expertise into operational decisions.

Meaning accuracyDoes the Hausa preserve the intended meaning, nuance, tense, entities, and idioms?
Natural fluencyDoes it sound like natural Hausa for the context?
Tone/registerIs the tone suitable for the user, product, domain, and social situation?
Cultural fitDoes it respect Hausa and Nigerian context, names, places, family, religion, and social norms?
Hallucination riskDoes it invent facts, overclaim certainty, or mislead in fluent Hausa?
SafetyIs it responsible around medical, legal, financial, emergency, and sensitive domains?
Instruction followingDid it follow the task, language, format, constraints, and requested structure?
Dataset usefulnessCan the example be trusted for training, evaluation, annotation, or QA?
